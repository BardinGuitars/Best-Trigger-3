New here? Read sections 3 and 4: in ten minutes you can replace or reinforce drum hits with samples. Sections 6 and 10 explain how to get the result just right, and section 11 how to leave the routine work to the automation. Names of buttons and menu items are written as they appear in the plugin.

1. About the plugin

Best Trigger 3 is a drum trigger. It listens to a recorded drum track (kick, snare, tom), finds every hit and at that very moment plays your samples: instead of the original sound or together with it.

  • Accurate hit detection by threshold, with protection against double triggering; the detector's filters do not change the sound.
  • Up to six samples at once, each with its own level, pan, pitch (Tune), envelope (ASR), filters (FLT), Mute/Solo and phase flip.
  • Multisamples: velocity layers and several round-robin takes; our own .bt3 format.
  • An oscilloscope: you see the original, each sample and their sum; the threshold, Retrigger and each sample's Offset are dragged with the mouse.
  • Phase tools: the Sum gain meter, automatic alignment from the music, phase tracking on every hit.
  • The Auto menu with Full Auto: sets the threshold, aligns the phase and switches phase tracking on.
  • A | B, 5-step Undo/Redo, input and output meters, master output level.
  • A sample browser with search and audition; migration of .tci libraries to .bt3.
  • MIDI: record hits as a MIDI clip and trigger samples with a note.

How to activate

  1. On the first start the plugin shows the activation window. Until the plugin is activated the window cannot be closed and the samples are silent.
  2. Enter the key (in the free version it is already filled in) and press Activate. An internet connection is needed once.
  3. Afterwards the bottom right corner says Licensed (or Free version (activated)).

Licence rules (full version)

  • One key, one computer. To move the licence press Deactivate this computer in the licence window (the button in the bottom right corner) and activate the same key on the new computer.
  • The plugin works offline on the permission it has stored (valid for a year). At every start, and about every 12 hours while there is a connection, it checks with the server.
  • If a licence is revoked (for example after a refund) or moved to another computer, the plugin loses it at the next check and shows the activation window.
  • A Best Trigger 3 licence covers all 3.x updates.
What is sent to the serverOn activation and checks: the key, a hash of the computer (not its serial number), the name and version of the DAW and plugin, the operating system and the country (from the connection). Usage statistics and error reports are sent only if you agree (a question at the first start; you can change it in the gear menu: Help improve Best Trigger). Your audio and file names are never sent.

3. Installation

Windows

  1. Download the installer from Releases: BestTrigger3-Free-…-Setup.exe or BestTrigger3-…-Setup.exe.
  2. Run it. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" window because the installer is not signed with a paid certificate. Click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Close your DAW before installing and rescan plugins afterwards. The plugin is installed in C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3.
  4. Insert the plugin on a drum track as an effect (insert).

macOS

The Mac version comes without Apple notarisation, so macOS will warn you the first time. This is expected; see how to allow the plugin.

TipThe plugin works best on a separate track of a single drum (kick, snare, tom) where the hits are clearly audible.

4. Quick start

  1. If you also want to hear the original drum, raise the Original level to 0 dB. By default it is at −∞ and only the samples are heard.
  2. Put samples in. In the free version they are already on six channels. In the full version drop a file on the plate at the bottom of a channel, right-click the plate, or open the Browser. A left click on a plate plays the sample: the further right you click, the harder the hit.
  3. Start playback in your DAW and choose Auto → Full Auto. The plugin sets the threshold, aligns the phase and switches phase tracking on. If something is not right, everything can be set by hand: read on.

The quick start guide with three pictures is shown on the first start; you can open it again: gear → Quick start guide….

6. How hits are detected

The plugin watches the loudness of the track. When the signal rises above the Threshold, it registers a hit, measures its strength and plays the samples. After that the detector "closes" and is ready for the next hit only after the Retrigger time has passed and the signal has dropped low enough (Sensitivity).

These parameters are set with the mouse right on the scope in Timeline mode. A double-click on a value box lets you type a number.

ParameterMeaningDefault
ThresholdThe level above which the signal counts as a hit (−60…0 dB). In Timeline: two red dashed lines.−30 dB
SensitivityHow quickly the detector re-arms after a hit: at 0% at 80% of the threshold (fast, catches rolls, but may fire twice on one hit), at 100% at 20% (safer against doubles, but fast hits may be missed). In Timeline: yellow re-arm lines.50%
RetriggerThe "dead time" after a hit (5–250 ms). In Timeline: the purple zone; drag its right edge.40 ms
Low Cut / High CutFilters for the detector only (they do not change the sound): they help to ignore rumble and hiss. The button at the bottom left. Low Cut 20…2000 Hz, High Cut 500…20000 Hz.20 / 12 000 Hz

Hit strength and Dynamics

The hit strength is measured from the peak in the first 1.5 ms after the threshold is crossed: from the threshold (soft) to 0 dB (loud). The Dynamics knob sets how much the hit affects the sound:

  • 1.00: the choice of layer and the loudness follow the hit strength fully;
  • 0: always the hardest layer at full level;
  • values in between give a smooth compromise.

Timeline mode

  1. Make sure the button on the right panel says Timeline (lit; it is the starting view) and start playback.
  2. The mouse wheel changes the window from 0.1 to 10 seconds; Hold freezes the picture (the wheel still zooms, the middle button scrolls).
  3. Drag the red lines so that real hits cross them and unwanted sounds stay inside.
  4. Grey/red "crosses" are sounds the plugin skipped because of Retrigger. If real hits are marked, lower Retrigger or Sensitivity. If one hit has several orange lines, raise them.

Fit fits the height of the picture to the signal; Ctrl+wheel changes it by hand.

A tip about a gateIf the track has a lot of bleed from other drums, for the best result of the automatic phase tools and a clean threshold it is recommended to put a gate before Best Trigger: it removes tails and unwanted sounds so the plugin sees clean hits.

7. Samples and mixer

Putting a sample on a channel (full version)

  • Drop a file on the plate of the channel; files dropped on the window beside a plate fill the first free slots.
  • Right-click the plate: Load sample or .bt3 file…, Load folder (multisample)…, Save multisample file (.bt3)…, Clear sample.
  • Browser: choose a file in the library tree (section 9).
  • WAV, AIFF, FLAC (mono or stereo, up to 120 seconds), multisample folders and .bt3 files are supported.

On every hit all loaded samples play at once, each with its own settings, so you can layer a "click" and a "sub" of a kick.

In the free version the plates hold the built-in samples; a right-click offers Restore the original sample or Clear sample.

The plate: auditioning

A left click on a plate with a sample plays only that sample the way it will sound on a hit: with all its settings (level, Pan, Tune, ENV, FLT, Offset, Ø, Mute/Solo, Humanize). The hit strength depends on where you click on the plate: further left is softer, further right is harder. A multisample plays the matching layer; single samples (one layer) always sound the same. The ▶ on the plate hints that it can be clicked. The name is shown without the extension; the full name pops up when you hold the cursor still.

The TEST strip on the scope

On the right of the scope (in both modes) there is a narrow green strip. No control works there except the left mouse button: it fires a test hit of all samples at once through the whole signal path. The strength depends on the height of the click: higher is harder, lower is softer (on multisamples this chooses the layer).

8. Multisamples and .bt3 files

A multisample is an instrument made of several files: layers by hit strength and several takes (round robin) in each. A take is chosen at random (repeats are possible); the layer by the hit strength.

Naming the files in a folder

Load the folder: right-click the plate → Load folder (multisample)…. Files are named like Name_R1_V0.wav (the order Name_V0_R1.wav is accepted too):

  • R + number: the take (round robin) number. It is for your convenience; takes play at random.
  • V + number: the MIDI velocity (0–127) from which this layer plays. Example: _V0, _V50, _V105, _V127 means: V0 plays from 0 to 49, V50 from 50 to 104, V105 from 105 to 126, and V127 only at exactly 127.
  • You can give levels instead of exact velocities, like in Trigger 2: _V1, _V2, _V3, _V4 (all numbers below 32). The plugin spreads them evenly over 0…127 in ascending order: a bigger number means a harder hit.
  • If no file matches such a name, all the files of the folder become takes of one layer.
CarefulSome libraries number them the other way round: V1 is the hardest layer. Then the multisample plays "upside down". Rename the files (or swap the numbers) so that a bigger number means a harder hit.

The .bt3 file

A multisample (even a single sample) can be saved into one .bt3 file: right-click the plate → Save multisample file (.bt3)…. The samples inside are compressed losslessly (FLAC), so the file is smaller, and it cannot be opened by just renaming it. The whole file is loaded into memory; nothing is read from disk while you play.

How layers relate to audio and MIDI

For audio the hit strength comes from the signal peak, for MIDI from the note velocity; in both cases the Dynamics knob sets how much it affects the choice of layer (section 6).

9. Library, browser and .tci migration

Full version only.

The library folder

Gear → Sample library → Choose sample library folder… sets the folder where you keep your samples. By default it is Documents\SSM\Best Trigger 3\Library. The file dialogs open in this folder. Also there: Open library folder and Use the default folder.

The sample browser

The Browser button in the right panel opens a separate window: a tree of the library's folders and files, a search box (all typed words must appear in the name and path), the Audition button (play on click), and the buttons Refresh, Migration…, Library folder…, Open in Explorer. At the bottom are six pads, one per sample channel.

  • One click on a file plays it (if Audition is on).
  • A double-click loads the file into the next free channel (when all six are taken, into the selected one); dragging onto a pad loads it into that pad. Click a pad to select it.
  • Right-click a file or folder: choose a pad and load (a folder loads as a multisample); right-click a pad to clear the channel.

Online libraries

The Online libraries… button in the browser opens a list of sample libraries that can be downloaded: for each one you see the size, a description and what is inside. Choose a library and press Download and install: the plugin downloads the archive, checks its checksum and unpacks it into your library folder (the folder structure is kept, your own files are never overwritten). Under the list you also see the author's own text (Info.txt) in full, with its links and copyrights, and the cover picture. The library then shows in the browser tree. Only sounds, .bt3 files and notes are taken from the archive.

Migrating a .tci library

Gear → Sample library → Migration (or the Migration… button in the browser) turns a library of Trigger Instrument Editor instruments (.tci) into .bt3 files:

  1. The plugin shows a warning: only free samples and samples you made yourself may be converted; you are responsible for having the right to convert them. Continue becomes available when you tick the confirmation.
  2. Choose the folder with the .tci library.
  3. The plugin creates .bt3 files straight in your library folder, repeating the names and the folder structure of the original. Ordinary audio files (WAV, AIFF, FLAC) lying in the same library are copied as they are.
  4. Running it again continues the work: finished files are skipped. A detailed report is saved in MIGRATION_LOG.txt.
Protected librariesOfficial paid libraries (the "INSTRUMENT 2" format) are recognised by their header and are never converted: such files are skipped. Your original files are not changed or deleted.

10. Phase and alignment

When a sample plays together with the original, their waves add up. If the peaks coincide the sum is fuller; if one goes "up" while the other goes "down" they cancel each other and the sound gets thin (especially in the lows). Aligning the phase means shifting the sample in time and, if needed, flipping its polarity.

Hit mode

With the button on Phase, the scope shows the last hit (from 40 ms before the hit to 85 ms after). The blue trace is the original, the coloured ones are samples, the white one is the sum (the Sum tick): what really goes to the output, with levels, Mute/Solo, Offset, Ø and envelopes. The traces are drawn with thickened lines, and the height fits the largest trace by itself.

Offset: shifting a sample against the original

Each sample has its own Offset from −30 to +30 ms (the original is the reference). The Smp 1…6 boxes at the top of the scope show the values.

  1. Click the box of the sample (it lights up).
  2. Drag the graph left and right: the Offset of the selected sample moves; with Shift ten times finer.
  3. A double-click on the box lets you type a number; a double-click on the graph resets the Offset to 0.

Sum gain

In the top right corner of the scope is the Sum gain number: how much louder the sum is than the channels added without regard to phase (dB).

Colour and valueMeaning
Green, from +1.5 dBThe waves are in phase; the sum is fuller. The goal.
Yellow, about 0 dBThe phase relation is neutral.
Red, below −1.5 dBThe channels cancel each other: move the sample or press Ø.

The number is calculated over the first 40 ms of the hit (in Bass mode over the first 100 ms, lows only). If the original is at −∞ but its tick is on, it is still counted in the on-screen sum.

Aligning by hand

  1. Leave the Original and one sample on the screen.
  2. If the first waves go in opposite directions, press Ø.
  3. Switch on Sum and move the Offset until Sum gain is highest and green.
  4. Check on several hits (soft and hard); for live recordings switch on tracking (section 11).

Phase tracking

Auto → Track phase on every hit: before a sample starts, the plugin looks at the first 10–12 ms of this very hit and adjusts the start within ±2 ms. The plugin latency does not grow because of it.

LimitsTracking makes no sense if the original is "dirty" (a lot of bleed from other drums): then put a gate in front of the plugin (section 6) or use the one-off Align phase. With a strongly negative Offset (below about −16 ms) tracking is switched off on such hits.

11. The Auto menu

While an automatic tool is listening to the music, the Auto button blinks and shows the number of hits heard (for example 5/24); progress also shows in the status line. A result can be undone: Undo in the status line, the item Undo the last auto change, or Undo in the top panel.

ItemWhat it does
Full Auto: threshold, phaseEverything in order: 1) listens to transients for 12 seconds and sets Threshold and Retrigger; 2) collects ~24 hits and finds the Offset and Ø of each sample; 3) switches phase tracking on. The sample levels are not changed. The whole Full Auto is undone with one press of Undo.
Whole signal / BassWhich part of the signal the phase and level analyses and the Sum gain meter use. For a kick choose Bass (default), for a snare and cymbals Whole signal.
Align phase: listen to the musicListens to playback until about 24 hits are collected (40 seconds at most) and finds the Offset (±30 ms) and Ø of each loaded sample. The result and the confidence in per cent are written in the status line; with low confidence (< 30%) the plugin warns you.
Track phase on every hitThe phase tracking switch (section 10).
Match samples to the originalSets each sample's level from the loudness of the original (first 100 ms: for Whole signal with K-weighting, as in LUFS; for Bass the lows below 250 Hz).
Make samples equally loudEvens the samples out against each other: the loudest stays at 0 dB. No music is needed.
Learn Threshold and RetriggerListens to all transients for 12 seconds, separates real hits from quiet bleed and sets Threshold and Retrigger. If hits and bleed cannot be told apart, the plugin says so: then check the threshold by hand in Timeline.
Start playback automatically when learningTries to start the DAW's playback by itself. It does not work in all DAWs: VST3 usually does not allow a plugin to press Play.
Sound is neededWhile playback is stopped the plugin says "Waiting for audio…" and starts counting when sound arrives. If there is no sound for 90 seconds the learning is cancelled.

13. MIDI

MIDI output

Every detected hit is also sent as a MIDI note (36 by default) with a velocity that matches the hit strength. Choose the note: gear → MIDI out note (with General MIDI names). This makes the plugin an "audio to MIDI converter".

Recording hits to a clip

  1. Press REC MIDI and start playback.
  2. Press REC MIDI again to stop.
  3. A green handle drag MIDI clip to DAW appears next to it: drag it onto a MIDI track.

If the DAW tells the plugin its position and tempo, the notes land on the bar grid; otherwise the clip is recorded at 120 BPM from the first hit.

MIDI input

To trigger samples with a MIDI note choose gear → Trigger source → MIDI and a note in MIDI in note (36 by default). The audio detector is off in this mode; the note velocity sets the hit strength.

15. Plugin latency

So that a sample can be placed earlier than the original (a negative Offset down to −30 ms) and so that the plugin has time to look at the hit, the original is delayed inside the plugin. The total latency is about 31.5 ms (30 ms of headroom plus 1.5 ms to measure the strength). The plugin reports it to the DAW, which compensates for it automatically.

Playing in real timeIf you play and monitor through the plugin, the delay will be noticeable. For live playing monitor around the plugin or record without it.
